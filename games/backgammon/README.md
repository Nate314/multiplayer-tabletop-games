# Backgammon

Race your fifteen checkers home and bear them off before your opponent.

Exactly two players. The first to join plays white and the second plays red. The opening roll decides who moves first.

## Rules

- Standard backgammon, a single money game played for the stake on the doubling cube
- Both players start with fifteen checkers in the standard layout
- Opening roll: once both players are seated, each rolls one die, again on a tie. The higher roller moves first using both numbers, so the opening move is never a double. The status line shows both rolls
- Every later turn starts with the player clicking Roll, or offering to double first. Each die moves one checker that many points, and doubles are played four times
- A checker may land on an empty point, your own point, or a point with one opposing checker (a blot). Landing on a blot hits it and sends it to the bar. A point with two or more opposing checkers is blocked
- A checker on the bar must enter in your opponent's home board before you move anything else
- You must use both dice if you can. If only one can be used, you must use the higher one when possible. If you cannot use your roll at all, you forfeit it, the turn passes and the status line says so
- Once all fifteen checkers are in your home board you may bear off. A die bears off from the point with its number, and a higher die bears off from your highest occupied point. When either die could bear off the same checker, you choose which one to use
- Doubling cube: the cube starts in the middle at 1. Before rolling on your turn, you may offer to double if the cube is in the middle or you own it. Your opponent either takes, which doubles the cube and gives them its ownership, so only they may redouble next, or drops, which concedes the game at the cube's value before the double. There is no limit on redoubles
- The first player to bear off all fifteen checkers wins the cube's value: once for a single game, twice for a gammon (the loser bore off none) and three times for a backgammon (the loser also still has a checker on the bar or in the winner's home board)
- If a player leaves mid-game, the other player wins
- Either player can ask to undo the last move, including a roll, a double, a take or a drop. It is undone only if the other player approves

## Controls

- Each player sees the board from their own side, with their home board at the bottom right and points numbered 1 to 24 from that side
- At the start of your turn, click Roll, or Double to offer a double first. When your opponent doubles, click Take or Drop
- After rolling, the checkers that can move are ringed. Click one, then click one of its highlighted points, or your tray on the right to bear off
- When either die could bear off the checker, buttons under the board ask which die to use. Cancel picks a different checker
- Or drag a checker onto one of its highlighted points, or onto your tray to bear off, with the mouse or a finger; drop it anywhere else to put it back. The bear-off die buttons still appear when either die would do
- Click the selected checker again, or anywhere else, to pick a different one
- Each click moves one checker by one die. This turn's dice are shown beside the board and used dice are faded
- The cube's value and owner are shown beside the board, and the points won once the game is over
- The board is dimmed when it is not your turn, and the last checker move is outlined
- Click Undo to ask your opponent to undo the last move
- Click Rules at the top of the page for these rules, and FAQ in it for a short guide to the rules and controls

## Differences from Wikipedia

Only standard money-game rules are played. These optional rules described by Wikipedia are not offered:

- Automatic doubles when the opening roll is a tie
- Beavers and raccoons (redoubling immediately while keeping the cube)
- The Jacoby rule (gammons and backgammons count only after the cube has been turned)
- Match play, with the Crawford and Holland rules
