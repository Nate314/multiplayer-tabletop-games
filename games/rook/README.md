# Rook

Bid for the nest, name trump and take counters in tricks with your partner, first team to the target score (500 unless the host chose another), for 4 players, or 4 to 7 when the host ticks "I know what I'm doing". This is the Kentucky Discard version, which Wikipedia calls Tournament Rook.

## Rules

- **Teams.** Four players in two partnerships, partners sitting across: seats 1 and 3 against seats 2 and 4. With 6 players the host's "Teams at 6 players" sets the teams: two teams of three, seats 1, 3 and 5 against 2, 4 and 6 (the standard), or three teams of two, partners sitting three apart: 1 and 4, 2 and 5, 3 and 6. Teams are fixed for the game. With 5 or 7 players there are no fixed teams: each player scores on their own, and each hand the bidder calls partners (see Calling partners)
- **Seats.** Play goes around in seat order: seat 1, then 2, and so on, and the last seat is followed by seat 1
- **Deck.** 41 cards: 5 to 14 in red, yellow, green and black, and the Rook. In each color 14 is high and 5 is low
- **Deal.** The last seat (seat 4 with four players) deals the first hand and the deal passes to the next seat after each hand. Five cards go face down to the nest and each player gets 9. With more players the hands are smaller so the 41 cards deal evenly: 5 players get 7 each and a 6-card nest, 6 players 6 each and a 5-card nest, 7 players 5 each and a 6-card nest
- **Counters.** Each 5 is worth 5 points, each 10 and 14 is worth 10, and the Rook 20: 120 points in a hand
- **Bidding.** Starting left of the dealer and going around, each player bids or passes. Bids go from 70 to 120 in steps of 5, and each must beat the last. A pass is final for that hand. The bidding ends when everyone else has passed, or at once on a bid of 120. If everyone passes, the next player deals a new hand
- **The nest.** The bid winner adds the nest to their hand, lays aside face down as many cards as the nest held (5 with four or six players), then names trump. Only the bidder sees the cards laid aside
- **Calling partners (5 and 7 players).** After naming trump the bidder calls one card with 5 players, two with 7, from the cards not in their own hand. Whoever holds a called card is the bidder's partner for this hand: 2 against 3 with 5 players, 3 against 4 with 7. The called cards are shown to everyone, but who holds one stays secret until the card is played. A called card may be one the bidder laid aside, so the bidder can play with fewer partners or alone
- **Tricks.** The player left of the dealer leads the first trick, and the winner of each trick leads the next. Follow the color led if you can; otherwise play any card. The highest trump takes the trick, else the highest card of the color led
- **The Rook** is the highest trump, whatever color is trump. It may be played at any time, even when you could follow suit. When the Rook is led, everyone must play trump if they can, and when trump is led a player whose only trump is the Rook must play it
- **Last trick.** Whoever takes the last trick also takes the cards laid aside and the counters in them
- **Scoring.** If the bidding team took at least its bid, it scores what it took; otherwise it loses the amount of its bid. The other team always scores what it took. With called partners (5 and 7 players) scores are kept per player: each member of the bidding side scores what the side took together if it made the bid, else loses the bid, and each defender scores what all the defenders took together. With three teams of two, "Defending teams score" sets how the two teams that did not win the bid score: each team its own counters (the standard), or together, each defending team scoring what both defending teams took. Scores can go below zero. A scoring screen shows the hand, and the next hand is dealt once every connected player has pressed Continue (disconnected players are not waited for)
- **Winning.** The first team to reach the target score (500 unless the host chose another) wins. If several teams reach it in the same hand the highest total wins; if the leading totals are equal another hand is played. With 5 and 7 players the first player to the target wins, and players with equal top totals share the win
- You see only your own hand and how many cards everyone else holds. The nest, the cards laid aside and the counters each team has taken stay hidden until the hand is scored; the number of tricks each team has taken is shown. Who holds a called card stays hidden until it is played; only its holder knows
- **Leaving.** A disconnected player keeps their seat and the game waits for them on their turn. A dismissed player breaks their partnership, so the game ends and the highest-scoring team without them wins (with four players, the other team; at 5 and 7 players, where partners are called each hand, the highest-scoring player left)
- Any player can ask to undo the last move. It is undone only if every connected player approves, and only while every seat is filled. Continue is not a move, so an undo goes back past it

## Controls

- When it is your turn to bid, choose an amount and click Bid, or click Pass
- If you win the bid, click as many cards as the nest held (5 with four or six players) in your hand to pick them (click again to put one back), then click Lay aside. Then click a color to name trump. With 5 or 7 players, then pick the card (or two cards) to call from the list and click Call
- In a trick, the cards you may play are raised and outlined; click one to play it
- The table shows each team's score and tricks, the hand number, the bid and trump, and the seats around the middle with you at the bottom (with four players, your partner across). Each seat shows its card count, the dealer and its bid or pass. The middle shows the nest until trump is named, then the trick being played, or the last trick taken with who took it
- The bidder sees the cards they laid aside below their hand
- With 5 or 7 players the table shows the called cards. If you hold one, it says you are the bidder's partner; once a called card is played, its holder's seat is marked partner
- After each hand, read the scoring screen and click Continue; after the last hand click Close, and Show Scoring opens it again
- Click Undo to ask everyone to undo the last move
- Click Rules at the top of the page for these rules, and FAQ in it for a short guide to the rules and controls

## Differences from Wikipedia

These follow the Official rules (Tournament Rook, also known as Kentucky Discard) in [Wikipedia's Rook article](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rook_(card_game)). What is different:

- **Target score.** Wikipedia plays to 300. This game plays to 500 unless the host chooses 300 or 1,000 before play starts (everyone must agree), 500 being the owner's choice and a common target. If both teams pass it in one hand, the higher total wins as in the article; the article does not cover an equal total, so another hand is played
- **No redeal for a pointless hand.** Wikipedia lets a player with no counters call a redeal during bidding. That is not offered here
- **Everyone passes.** The article does not say what happens if nobody bids. Here the next dealer deals a new hand
- **Dealing.** The article deals one card at a time with the nest built up between rounds. Here the shuffled deck is split straight into the nest and the hands, which gives the same chances. Who deals first and how the deal moves are not in the article: the last seat deals first and the deal passes to the next seat
- **More players.** The article is for four players. With "I know what I'm doing" ticked the host can seat up to 7. Six play in fixed teams, two teams of three or three teams of two as the host chooses, and with three teams the host also chooses how the two defending teams score. Five and seven play with called partners, which the article does not cover: the bidder may call a laid-aside card and play alone, and equal top totals share the win. The hand and nest sizes change so the deck deals evenly (see Deal), and the bid winner lays aside as many cards as the nest held
- **Reneging cannot happen.** The article has a penalty for not following suit when able. Here a card that does not follow suit when it must is refused, so there is no penalty
- **Reviewing tricks.** The article keeps taken tricks face down. Here the last trick taken stays in the middle until the next card is played, and the number of tricks each team has taken is shown
- **Leaving.** The rules do not cover a player leaving mid-game. Here the partnership is broken, so the game ends and the highest-scoring team without the dismissed player wins
