# Rook

Bid for the nest, name trump and take counters in tricks with your partner, first team to 500, for exactly 4 players. This is the Kentucky Discard version, which Wikipedia calls Tournament Rook.

## Rules

- **Teams.** Four players in two partnerships, partners sitting across: seats 1 and 3 against seats 2 and 4
- **Deck.** 41 cards: 5 to 14 in red, yellow, green and black, and the Rook. In each color 14 is high and 5 is low
- **Deal.** Seat 4 deals the first hand and the deal passes to the left after each hand. Five cards go face down to the nest and each player gets 9
- **Counters.** Each 5 is worth 5 points, each 10 and 14 is worth 10, and the Rook 20: 120 points in a hand
- **Bidding.** Starting left of the dealer and going around, each player bids or passes. Bids go from 70 to 120 in steps of 5, and each must beat the last. A pass is final for that hand. The bidding ends when everyone else has passed, or at once on a bid of 120. If all four pass, the next player deals a new hand
- **The nest.** The bid winner adds the nest to their hand, lays any 5 cards aside face down, then names trump. Only the bidder sees the cards laid aside
- **Tricks.** The player left of the dealer leads the first trick, and the winner of each trick leads the next. Follow the color led if you can; otherwise play any card. The highest trump takes the trick, else the highest card of the color led
- **The Rook** is the highest trump, whatever color is trump. It may be played at any time, even when you could follow suit. When the Rook is led, everyone must play trump if they can, and when trump is led a player whose only trump is the Rook must play it
- **Last trick.** Whoever takes the last trick also takes the cards laid aside and the counters in them
- **Scoring.** If the bidding team took at least its bid, it scores what it took; otherwise it loses the amount of its bid. The other team always scores what it took. Scores can go below zero. A scoring screen shows the hand, and the next hand is dealt once every connected player has pressed Continue (disconnected players are not waited for)
- **Winning.** The first team to reach 500 wins. If both teams reach it in the same hand the higher total wins; if the totals are equal another hand is played
- You see only your own hand and how many cards everyone else holds. The nest, the cards laid aside and the counters each team has taken stay hidden until the hand is scored; the number of tricks each team has taken is shown
- **Leaving.** A disconnected player keeps their seat and the game waits for them on their turn. A dismissed player breaks their partnership, so the game ends and the other team wins
- Any player can ask to undo the last move. It is undone only if every connected player approves, and only while all four seats are filled. Continue is not a move, so an undo goes back past it

## Controls

- When it is your turn to bid, choose an amount and click Bid, or click Pass
- If you win the bid, click 5 cards in your hand to pick them (click again to put one back), then click Lay aside. Then click a color to name trump
- In a trick, the cards you may play are raised and outlined; click one to play it
- The table shows both teams' scores and tricks, the hand number, the bid and trump, and the four seats with you at the bottom and your partner across. Each seat shows its card count, the dealer and its bid or pass. The middle shows the nest until trump is named, then the trick being played, or the last trick taken with who took it
- The bidder sees the cards they laid aside below their hand
- After each hand, read the scoring screen and click Continue; after the last hand click Close, and Show Scoring opens it again
- Click Undo to ask everyone to undo the last move
- Click FAQ at the top of the page for a short guide to the rules and controls

## Differences from Wikipedia

These follow the Official rules (Tournament Rook, also known as Kentucky Discard) in [Wikipedia's Rook article](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rook_(card_game)). What is different:

- **Target score.** Wikipedia plays to 300. This game plays to 500, the owner's choice and a common target. If both teams pass it in one hand, the higher total wins as in the article; the article does not cover an equal total, so another hand is played
- **No redeal for a pointless hand.** Wikipedia lets a player with no counters call a redeal during bidding. That is not offered here
- **Everyone passes.** The article does not say what happens if nobody bids. Here the next dealer deals a new hand
- **Dealing.** The article deals one card at a time with the nest built up between rounds. Here the shuffled deck is split straight into the nest and four hands, which gives the same chances. Who deals first and how the deal moves are not in the article: seat 4 deals first and the deal passes to the left
- **Reneging cannot happen.** The article has a penalty for not following suit when able. Here a card that does not follow suit when it must is refused, so there is no penalty
- **Reviewing tricks.** The article keeps taken tricks face down. Here the last trick taken stays in the middle until the next card is played, and the number of tricks each team has taken is shown
- **Leaving.** The rules do not cover a player leaving mid-game. Here the partnership is broken, so the game ends and the other team wins
