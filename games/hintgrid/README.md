# Hint Grid

Two teams, Red and Blue, race to find their words on a grid of 25 from one-word clues, for 4 to 8 players. It plays like the commercial game described in Wikipedia's [Codenames](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Codenames_(board_game)) article, with its own name, words and wording.

## Rules

- **Teams.** Each player is on Red or Blue. Each team has exactly one clue-giver, and every other player on it is a guesser. Each team needs a clue-giver and at least one guesser
- **Setting up.** In the lobby each player picks a team and a role, or the Even out button balances them. Once everyone is placed and each team has a clue-giver and a guesser, the first clue-giver can give a clue, and that starts the game. A player who joins before the first clue is unplaced until they pick, and the game waits for them
- **The grid.** 25 words, drawn from a built-in list of at least 400, face down in five rows. Behind them is a hidden key: 9 words for the team that starts, 8 for the other team, 7 bystanders and 1 trap. The team with 9 starts, and the clue-givers see the key
- **Clues.** On its turn the team's clue-giver types one word and a number from 1 to 9. The game refuses a clue that is a word on the grid, ignoring capitals, spaces, hyphens and apostrophes, and a clue of more than one word
- **Guessing.** After the clue, any guesser of that team taps a word to turn it over. The team gets up to the number plus one guesses. A word of their own team lets them guess on
- **Ending a turn.** A bystander or the other team's word ends the turn at once. So does using up the number plus one guesses. After at least one guess, the guessers can press End guessing to stop early
- **The trap.** Turning over the trap loses the game at once: the other team wins
- **Winning.** The first team to turn over all its words wins, even on the other team's turn. Standings show every player on the winning team as a winner
- **Hidden key.** Only the clue-givers see the key. Guessers see only the words already turned over, and a table screen and History do the same until the game ends. Spectators see the key
- **Leaving.** A disconnected player keeps their seat and can rejoin. A dismissed player loses their role. If a team is left without a clue-giver or without a guesser, the game ends and the team with fewer words left to find wins, or it is a draw if equal
- Any player can ask to undo the last move: a clue, a guess or End guessing. It is undone only if every connected player approves. Picking a team or role is not a move and cannot be undone

## Controls

- **Lobby.** Click Be Red's clue-giver, Guess for Red, Be Blue's clue-giver or Guess for Blue to pick, or Even out to balance the teams. Even out keeps any clue-giver already chosen, spreads the rest and names a clue-giver for a team that has none
- **Clue-giver.** Type the Clue, choose the Number and click Give clue. The key shows on each word as a mark: R for Red, B for Blue, a circle for a bystander and a cross for the trap
- **Guesser.** On your team's turn, after the clue, click a word to turn it over, and click End guessing to stop early
- **The table.** Shows the clue, the guesses left and the words left for each team. The last word turned over is outlined
- **Undo.** Click Undo to ask everyone to undo the last move
- Click Rules at the top of the page for these rules, and FAQ in it for a short guide to the rules and controls

## Differences from Wikipedia

These follow the rules in [Wikipedia's Codenames article](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Codenames_(board_game)). What is different:

- **Original names and words.** Red and Blue teams, clue-givers, guessers, bystanders and one trap. The word list is this project's own
- **Choosing teams.** The article does not say how players are split. Here each player picks a team and a role in the lobby, or Even out balances them, and the first clue can be given once each team has a clue-giver and a guesser and everyone is placed
- **Clues.** The article lets the clue-giver speak. Here the clue is typed: one word and a number from 1 to 9, with no zero or unlimited clue. A clue that is a grid word is refused by the game, and the turn goes on after the refusal, where the article ends the turn and lets the other team turn over a word
- **Guessing.** Any guesser of the team may tap, and at least one guess is needed before End guessing, as in the article. The article does not say who taps
- **No timer.** The article mentions an optional timer. There is none here
- **Leaving.** The article does not cover a player leaving. Here a dismissed player ends the game when a team is left without a clue-giver or a guesser, and the team with fewer words left wins
- **Seeing the key.** The article has the clue-givers look at a key card. Here spectators see the key too, and a table screen does not
- **Undo.** The article has no undo. Here a clue, a guess or End guessing can be undone if everyone approves
