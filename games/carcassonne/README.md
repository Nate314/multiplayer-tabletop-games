# Carcassonne

Lay land tiles to build a countryside of cities, roads, monasteries and fields, and claim them with your meeples, for 2 to 5 players.

## Rules

- **Tiles.** The 72 land tiles of the base game in their published counts: a city on every side (1, with a pennant), a city with a straight road (4, one of them the start tile), a city on one side (5), a city across the middle (2 with a pennant, 1 without), two cities on opposite sides (3) or neighbouring sides (2), a city with a road curving left (3) or right (3), a city with a junction (3), a city across a corner (2 with a pennant, 3 without), the same with a curved road (2 with a pennant, 3 without), a city on three sides (1 with a pennant, 3 without), the same with a road (2 with a pennant, 1 without), a straight road (8), a curved road (9), a three-way junction (4), a crossroads (1), a monastery (4) and a monastery with a road (2). There are 10 pennants. The start tile is on the board and the other 71 are shuffled face down
- **Turn order.** The first player to join goes first, and turns go up through the seats
- **Placing a tile.** The tile you must place is drawn for you and shown to everyone. It goes next to at least one tile on the board, turned any way, so that every side touching a tile matches it: city to city, road to road, field to field
- **A tile that fits nowhere** is set aside out of the game and another is drawn, as the current official rules say. The count of set-aside tiles is shown
- **Placing a meeple.** Each player has 7 meeples. After placing the tile you may put one meeple on one of its features: a city, a road, a field (a farmer) or the monastery. The whole road, city or field it joins must have no meeple on it yet. Features claimed separately can later be joined by a tile, and are then shared
- **Scoring during play.** Right after the meeple step, every feature the tile finished scores for the players with the most meeples on it, ties all scoring in full, and those meeples go back to their players. A road is finished when both ends stop at a junction, a city, a monastery, or by closing a loop, and scores 1 point a tile. A city is finished when its walls close and scores 2 points a tile and 2 a pennant. A monastery is finished when all eight squares around it hold tiles and scores 9
- **End of the game.** The game ends when the last tile is placed (or the last tiles fit nowhere). Then every feature still claimed scores for the players with the most meeples on it: an unfinished road 1 a tile, an unfinished city 1 a tile and 1 a pennant, an unfinished monastery 1 plus 1 for each tile around it, and each field 3 points for every finished city it touches. The highest total wins; tied players share the win
- Nothing is hidden but the order of the tiles still to draw. Everyone sees the board, the tile being placed, every score and every player's meeples, and how many tiles are left
- A dismissed player scores nothing more. Their meeples stay on the board and still hold their features, so nobody can put a meeple on those features directly, but they count for nobody when a feature scores and go back to the supply when it does. If they had placed their tile and not yet chosen a meeple, their turn ends with no meeple (anything the tile finished still scores for the others) and the next player draws; if they had not placed it, the next player places that same tile. The last player left wins
- Any player can ask to undo the last move. Placing a tile and choosing a meeple are separate moves. A move is undone only if every player still in the game approves

## Controls

- On your turn the board highlights every square where the drawn tile fits. Click one to see the tile there; Turn left and Turn right then try only the turns that fit that square. Before you pick a square the Turn buttons simply turn the tile in the side panel. Click Place tile to put it down
- You can also drag the tile from the side panel onto a highlighted square (mouse or touch). Dropping only picks the square, as a click does, so you can still turn the tile; Place tile puts it down. Dropped anywhere else, the tile goes back
- The board then shows your placed tile up close, with a circle on every feature that can take a meeple. Click one (click it again to change your mind) and then Place meeple, or click No meeple. Farmers lie on their side
- Or drag the meeple from the tray under the tile onto a circle. Dropping only picks that circle, as a click does; Place meeple puts it down
- The half-finished turn is part of the game: after a refresh you see your tile placed and the meeple step still waiting
- Drag the board to pan it, use the mouse wheel or the + and − buttons to zoom, and Fit to see the whole board. The tile placed last is outlined
- The side panel shows the tile being placed, how many tiles are left, and the scores, highest first, with each player's meeples in hand. At the end a table shows what the final scoring added for roads, cities, monasteries and fields
- Click Undo to ask everyone to undo the last move
- With a table screen connected, on Hand only (the default) the other players are hidden and the board shows only on your turn
- Click Rules at the top of the page for these rules, and FAQ in it for a short guide to the rules and controls
- With Color-blind symbols on in Settings, each meeple color also shows its shape: red circle, blue square, yellow star, green triangle, black diamond. Meeples on the board, the meeple you are about to place and the player list show it

## Differences from Wikipedia

These follow the rules described on [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carcassonne_(board_game)) and, where it is silent, the current official Hans im Glück and Z-Man rules (the third edition scoring, which Wikipedia also describes). What remains different:

- **Base game only.** No expansions, no river, no large meeple or abbot, and no sixth player
- **Who goes first.** The rules have the youngest player start. Here the first player to join does
- **A tile that fits nowhere.** Wikipedia lets the players either remove it from the game or put it back in the pile. Here it is always set aside out of the game and the next tile is drawn, as the current official rules say, without asking the players to agree
- **The eighth meeple.** The box has eight meeples a player, one used on the score track. Here the score is shown in the side panel and every player has the 7 that can be played
- **Two moves a turn.** At a table the tile and meeple are one action. Here placing the tile and choosing the meeple are separate moves, so each can be undone on its own and a refresh keeps the half-finished turn
- **Leaving.** The rules do not cover a player leaving. Here a dismissed player's meeples stay on the board, hold their features and count for nobody, and their turn passes on as described above
