# Corners

Place polyomino pieces so that your pieces touch only at corners, and cover the most board, for 2 to 4 players on a Squares board (a 20 by 20 grid) or, when the host chooses it, a Triangles board (a hexagon of triangles).

## Rules

- **Boards.** The Board house rule picks Squares (the standard) or Triangles. Squares is a 20 by 20 grid and each color has 21 pieces of 1 to 5 squares. Triangles is a hexagon of side 9 (486 triangles) and each color has the 22 shapes of 1 to 6 triangles
- **Colors.** There are four colors, Blue, Yellow, Red and Green, and they take turns in that order
- **Two players.** Each player plays two colors: Alice has Blue and Red, Bob has Yellow and Green
- **Three players.** Each player plays one color. The fourth color, Green, is shared: the three players take turns placing it, and it scores for nobody
- **Four players.** Each player plays one color
- **First piece.** On Squares, a color's first piece must cover its own corner of the board: Blue top left, Yellow top right, Red bottom right, Green bottom left. On Triangles, a color's first piece must cover one of the six starting points, each 3 steps in from a corner of the hexagon toward the middle, and the dots on the board mark them. Any color may take any starting point not yet covered
- **Later pieces.** Each later piece must touch a piece of the same color at a corner, and must never touch one along a side. It may touch other colors anywhere. On Triangles a corner touching the middle of a straight side of the same color counts as a corner touch. Pieces turn and flip, and each piece is used once
- **Passing.** A color with no legal place for any piece it has left passes for the rest of the game, and the game says so. When no color can place, the game ends
- **Scoring.** Each color loses one point for every square or triangle in the pieces it did not place. A color that places all its pieces scores 15 instead, or 20 if its last piece was the single square or triangle. A player's score is the total of their colors. The highest score wins, and a tie for the top is a shared win
- **Leaving.** A dismissed player's colors stop placing and their pieces stay on the board. With three players the shared color keeps going round the players who are left. The platform ends a game left with one player

## Controls

- On your turn, click a piece in Your pieces to pick it up, and click it again to put it back
- Click Turn left or Turn right to turn it and Flip to flip it. On a keyboard, Q turns it left, E turns it right, F flips it and Escape puts it back
- Every place the piece can go is outlined on the board. With a mouse, the piece shows under the pointer and a click places it. You can also click Place while the piece is showing
- On a phone, tap a piece, tap an outlined place to show the piece there, then tap the same place again to place it. Tapping a place where the piece cannot go places nothing
- Turning or flipping keeps the piece where it is when it still fits there, and otherwise drops the preview
- The panel shows whose turn it is and which color they place, each color's owner and score, and each player's total. A color that has gone out is marked
- Click Undo to ask everyone to undo the last move
- Turn on Color-blind symbols in Settings to show a shape on every placed piece: Blue square, Yellow star, Red circle, Green triangle
- Click Rules at the top of the page for these rules, and FAQ in it for a short guide to the rules and controls

## Differences from Wikipedia

These follow the rules in [Wikipedia's Blokus article](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blokus). What is different:

- **Three players.** Wikipedia lets a three-player game either give one player two colors or place the fourth color "in a non-strategic way". Here the three players take turns placing the shared fourth color, which scores for nobody
- **The triangle board.** Wikipedia does not give the triangle board's size, piece set or starting points. Here the board is a hexagon of side 9 (486 triangles), each color has the 22 shapes of 1 to 6 triangles, and there are six starting points 3 steps in from each corner of the hexagon toward the middle. Any color's first piece may cover any starting point not yet covered
- **Colors on the triangle board.** Wikipedia's triangle game is best with three colors. Here the Triangles board uses four colors at every player count, with the same two-colors and shared-color rules as Squares
- **Passing.** Colors with no legal placement pass automatically, and the game says so. There is no Pass button
- **Playing on screen.** Pieces cannot be placed illegally and the score is counted for you
