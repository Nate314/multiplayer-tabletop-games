# Charades

Act out a prompt on camera while everyone else races to guess it in the chat, for 2 to 10 players. Each player acts 3 times, and the highest score wins.

## Rules

- **Cameras.** The site shows the prompt, the chat and the scores, but no video. Play where everyone can see the actor: in the same room or on the group's own video call
- **Turns.** Players act in seat order. Each turn has three parts: the actor chooses a prompt, acts it out while everyone else guesses, and then the prompt is shown to all
- **Choosing.** The actor is offered 3 prompts and picks one. Only the actor sees them. From the second turn on, if 20 seconds pass without a choice, one of them is chosen at random
- **Acting.** The actor has 90 seconds to act out the prompt, which shows in large type on their screen. Speaking, mouthing words and pointing at written letters are not allowed; gestures and mime are the only clue
- **Guessing.** Everyone else types guesses in the chat. A guess is correct when it holds the prompt as whole words, ignoring capitals, spaces, hyphens and apostrophes, so "are you riding a horse?" guesses "riding a horse". The correct guess itself is never shown; the chat says that you guessed the prompt
- **Blanks.** Guessers see a blank for each letter of the prompt, with its spaces and hyphens, so they know how many words it has and how long each is
- **So close!** A guess one letter away from the prompt (one letter added, missing or wrong) tells only the player who typed it that they are close
- **Guessed-only chat.** Once you have guessed the prompt, what you type during the acting is seen only by the actor and the others who have guessed, so nobody can give the prompt away. The actor cannot chat while acting
- **Reveal.** The turn ends when time runs out or when everyone has guessed. The prompt is shown to everyone for 5 seconds, along with the guessed-only chat of that turn, and the next player chooses a prompt
- **Scoring.** A correct guess scores 10 + 90 x the share of the acting time left, rounded: 100 for a guess at once, 55 halfway, 10 at the buzzer. The actor scores 25 for each player who guesses the prompt
- **Winning.** After every player has acted their turns, the highest score wins. Players with equal scores share the win
- **House rules.** The host can set the time per turn (30 seconds, 1 minute, 90 seconds, 2 minutes or 3 minutes), the prompt choices offered (3, 4 or 5) and the turns per player (1 to 5). Everyone must agree to them before play starts
- **Spectators.** A spectator sees the prompt once it is chosen and every chat line, including the guessed-only chat and each player's "So close!" lines
- **Table screen.** A table screen (a TV or tablet joined as Table screen) is in view of the players, so it gets only the blanks, the scores and the public chat. It never shows the prompt, the guessed-only chat or anyone's "So close!" lines until the reveal
- **Leaving.** A player who leaves keeps their seat and can rejoin. Dismissing the actor ends their turn and shows the prompt. Dismissing a guesser ends the turn when everyone left has guessed. If only one player is left, that player wins
- Any player can ask to undo. Undo takes back the last scored guess or turn change, and the chat goes back to that point. It is undone only if everyone else approves. As in every game, Undo is still offered after the game is over

## Controls

- The actor clicks one of the offered prompts to start acting
- Everyone else types guesses in the chat box and presses Enter
- Click Undo to ask the other players to undo the last move
- Click Rules at the top of the page for these rules, and FAQ in it for a short guide to the rules and controls

## Differences from Wikipedia

These follow the parlor game in [Wikipedia's Charades article](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charades), played online. What is different:

- **No teams.** The article plays two or more teams, each acting for its own teammates. Here everyone plays for themselves: all other players guess at once, and faster guesses score more
- **Prompts.** In the article the other team writes the answers, often titles of books, films, songs and plays. Here the game offers prompts it wrote (actions, animals, jobs, sports and everyday phrases), and the actor picks one
- **Chat guessing.** The article's guessers call out their guesses and the actor signals a correct one. Here guesses are typed in the chat and the game checks them, so nobody has to judge a guess
- **No signals.** The article's finger signals for the number of words and syllables, and its gestures for book, song, film, TV program, play, "sounds like", short words and "the", are not needed: the blanks show how many words the prompt has and how long each is. Players may still use any gesture they like
- **Cameras.** The article plays in one room. Here the acting happens on the group's own call or in the same room; the site has no video
- **Scoring.** The article scores a point per correct answer, and a point for each answer the other team fails to guess in time. Here a guess scores 10 + 90 x the share of the time left, and the actor 25 per player who guesses
- **Fixed turns.** The article plays until every player has acted at least once, and does not say how a winner is found. Here every player acts the same number of times (3 unless the host chose another), and the highest score wins
