# Rummikub

Lay down sets and runs of numbered tiles, rearrange the table, and be the first to empty your rack, for 2 to 4 players.

## Rules

- 106 tiles: the numbers 1 to 13 in four colors (black, blue, orange and red), two of each, and two jokers
- **Who goes first.** Each player draws a tile and the highest number goes first. A joker counts lowest, and tied players draw again. The tiles go back, the pool is shuffled and everyone takes 14 tiles. Play then passes in seat order
- **Groups.** A set is three or four tiles of one number, all in different colors. A run is three or more numbers in a row in one color. A run does not wrap: 13 is never followed by 1
- **First meld.** A player's first play must be new groups made only from their own tiles, together worth at least 30 points (each tile counts its number, a joker the tile it stands for). In that turn they may not add to or move tiles already on the table
- **Later turns.** After the first meld a turn may rearrange the table in any way: add to groups, split them, move tiles between them and merge them. At the end of the turn every group must be a valid set or run, and the player must have added at least one tile from their rack. A tile that was on the table may not go back to a rack
- **Jokers.** A joker stands for the tile its place in the group gives it: in a run, the number of its place; in a set, a missing color. A joker on the table may be taken by putting the tile it stands for in its place (in a set of three, either missing color), from the rack or from elsewhere on the table. The joker must then be used again on the table that turn
- **Drawing.** A player who does not play draws one tile from the pool, which ends their turn. With the pool empty, this is a pass
- **Winning and scoring.** The game is one round. The first player to empty their rack wins. Every other player loses the total of the tiles left on their rack, with a joker worth 30, and the winner gains the sum of those totals. If the pool is empty and every player passes in turn, the player with the lowest rack total wins and scores the same way; tied players share the win and each gains the others' totals
- You see only your own rack and how many tiles everyone else holds. The pool is hidden from everyone. While a player is rearranging, the others see the table as it was at the start of the turn
- A dismissed player's tiles go back into the pool, which is shuffled, and their turns are skipped. If the pool was empty, those tiles refill it and the passes start again. The last player left wins
- Any player can ask to undo the last move. It is undone only if every player still in the game approves

## Controls

- On your turn, click a tile on your rack or the table to pick it up (it lifts and is outlined), then click the tile it should go in front of, the + at the end of a group, or New group. Click a held tile again to put it down. You can also drag a tile, or a group by its handle, with the mouse or a finger and drop it in the same places; dropped anywhere else, it goes back. On a phone, drag a table tile or group sideways (a swipe up or down on the table scrolls the page), or pick it with a tap
- The handle at the start of a group picks up the whole group; put it at the end of another group to merge them. The thin bar between two tiles of a group splits the group there
- A tile you put on the table this turn, or a group made only of such tiles, can go back to your rack: drop it in front of a rack tile or click End of rack. Tiles that were on the table at the start of the turn cannot
- A group that is not a valid set or run has a dashed red border. The line under your rack says what is wrong with the play, or that it is ready. Click Play to send it
- Reset puts the table and your rack back as they were at the start of the turn. Draw a tile (Pass when the pool is empty) ends your turn and drops any changes
- Color and Number sort your rack. You can also move rack tiles about at any time, even on other players' turns, by clicking or by dragging a rack tile in front of another. Only you see the order, and it is kept from turn to turn
- Hover over a joker on the table to see what it stands for. The tiles the last player put down are outlined
- The side panel shows every player's tile count, a check for those who have made their first meld, the pool count and, at the end, each player's points
- With Color-blind symbols on in Settings, each color also shows its shape: black diamond, blue square, orange hexagon, red circle. Each numbered tile shows it in its top left corner; jokers have no color
- With Highlight newly drawn cards on in Settings (the default), the tile you draw is outlined in your rack until your move on your next turn, and follows the tile when you rearrange the rack
- Click Undo to ask everyone to undo the last move, and FAQ at the top of the page for a short guide

## Differences from Wikipedia

These follow the rules described on [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rummikub). What remains different:

- **No turn timer.** Wikipedia gives a limit of one or two minutes a turn. Here a turn takes as long as it takes
- **A whole round, not a running total.** The game is a single round with its scores shown. Wikipedia's scoring is for playing several rounds and adding them up
- **The lowest rack wins when nobody can play.** Wikipedia says the player with the fewest tiles wins when the pool is empty and nobody can play. Here the lowest rack total wins (published rule sheets differ on this point), and "nobody can play" is taken to mean every player has passed in turn with the pool empty
- **Drawing for the first turn.** Wikipedia says only that the highest number starts. Here a joker counts lowest and tied players draw again
- **When a joker counts as taken.** A joker counts as taken when, after the play, it stands for a different tile or is no longer with any of the tiles it was with. Wikipedia does not say how to read a group of one number tile and two jokers, which could be a run or a set; here it counts as a run
- **Where a freed joker goes.** Wikipedia says a joker taken from the table must be used in a new set that turn. Here it only has to stay on the table in a valid group, which may be one already there. Each taken joker needs its own replacing tile
- **Leaving.** The rules do not cover a player leaving mid-game. Here their tiles go back into the pool
