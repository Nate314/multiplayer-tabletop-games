# Naval Battle

Hide your fleet on a grid and sink the other fleet first, in the traditional pencil and paper game for two players.

## Players

- Exactly 2 players. The first player to join is seat 1 and fires first
- **Salvo** (house rule, off by default): each turn, fire one shot per ship you still have afloat. The host turns it on before play starts and both players must agree

## Rules

- **The grid.** Each player has a 10x10 grid, rows A to J from top to bottom and columns 1 to 10 from left to right, so a square is named like B7
- **The fleet.** Each player places one ship of each length: 5, 4, 3, 3 and 2. A ship lies across or down, inside the grid, and ships cannot overlap; they may touch. Ships have no names and are known by their length
- **Placing.** Both players place their fleets at the same time. A confirmed fleet cannot be changed, and the game refuses a fleet that breaks these rules. Play starts once both fleets are confirmed: "Both fleets are ready. Alice fires first"
- **Firing.** Players take turns, seat 1 first, firing one shot per turn at a square of the other grid they have not fired at yet
- **Results.** Each shot is answered: miss, hit, or, when it hits the last square of a ship, hit and sunk with the length named: "Alice fired at B7: sank a ship of length 3". A hit does not give another shot. Which squares a sunk ship covered is not shown, only its length
- **Salvo.** With the house rule on, a player fires as many shots as they have ships afloat, counted from their own fleet at the start of the turn, all at once. When fewer squares are left to fire at than ships afloat, they fire at all the squares that are left. Each shot's result is shown: "Alice fired at B7, C7 and D7: hit, miss, sank a ship of length 3"
- **Winning.** The first player to sink every ship of the other fleet wins: "Alice sank every ship. Alice wins!". Both fleets are then shown
- You see only your own fleet and the result of every shot. The other fleet stays hidden from you, from spectators (unless its owner allows them to see it), from the table screen and from History until the game is over
- **Undo.** Either player can ask to undo. Undo takes back the last turn's shots; placing a fleet cannot be undone. It is undone only if the other player approves
- **Resigning and draws.** Once a fleet is placed, a player may resign at any time and the other player wins. Either player may offer a draw; the other accepts or declines, and the offer lapses when they decline or fire
- **Leaving.** If a player disconnects, the game waits for them to rejoin. If they are dismissed, the other player wins

## Controls

- To place a ship, drag it from the tray onto your grid, or tap it and then tap a square. Its top or left end goes on the square under the pointer. A drop where the ship does not fit puts it back in the tray
- While a ship is dragged over a square where it fits, the squares it would take are marked
- Tap a placed ship to pick it, then tap an empty square to move it there; tap it again to put it down
- Rotate turns the picked ship about its top or left end. With no placed ship picked, it turns the ships in the tray between across and down
- Random placement places the whole fleet for you, and Clear takes every ship off the grid
- Confirm fleet sends your fleet once every ship is placed. Until the other player confirms, your fleet shows with "Waiting for <name>"
- Your fleet shows your ships and the other player's shots at them; Target shows your shots. A miss, a hit and a sunk ship are marked differently
- On your turn, tap a square of Target to fire at it. With Salvo, tap squares to mark them (tap a marked square to unmark it), then click Fire, which shows how many are marked of how many you fire, such as Fire (3 of 5)
- The side panel shows the status and the players, with the player to fire outlined
- Click Resign and then Confirm resign to resign; click Keep playing to change your mind. Click Offer draw to offer a draw; Accept draw and Decline appear for the other player
- Click Undo to ask the other player to undo the last turn's shots
- History shows both grids after each turn, with the turn's shots marked, and the other fleet only once the game is over
- Click Rules at the top of the page for these rules, and FAQ in it for a short guide to the rules and controls

## Differences from Wikipedia

These follow the traditional pencil and paper game in [Wikipedia's article on the traditional grid naval game](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battleship_(game)). What is different:

- **Ship names.** The article names each ship and has the owner name the ship that was hit. Here ships are known by length only: a hit is not named, and a sinking names the length
- **Touching ships.** The article does not say whether ships may touch. Here they may
- **No extra shot.** A hit does not give another shot. The article gives none either; it is stated so nobody expects one
- **Salvo results.** The article also allows telling only how many shots hit. Here each shot's result is shown. When fewer squares are left than ships afloat, you fire at all that are left
- **Who starts.** The article does not say who fires first. Here seat 1, the first player to join, does
- **Placing.** Both players place at the same time on screen, with Random placement to place a whole fleet, and the game refuses an illegal fleet instead of trusting the player
- **Other variants.** The article's other variants (a fixed salvo of five shots, shots equal to the largest undamaged ship, not announcing a sinking, moving a ship) are not offered
- **The end.** Both fleets are shown once the game is over
- **Resigning, draws and leaving.** The article does not cover them. As in Chess, a player may resign, the players may agree to a draw, and if a player leaves and is dismissed the other player wins
