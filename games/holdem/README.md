# Texas Hold'em

No-limit Texas Hold'em with play chips: two private cards each, five shared cards, and four rounds of betting, for 2 to 8 players (up to 10 with the house rules override).

## Rules

- **Chips.** Everyone starts with 1,000 play chips. The blinds are 10 and 20 and never go up
- **Button and blinds.** The first player to join has the dealer button, and it moves to the next player with chips each hand. The player left of the button posts the small blind and the next player the big blind. Heads-up, the button posts the small blind and the other player the big blind. A player who cannot cover a blind posts what they have and is all in
- **The deal.** Each player with chips is dealt two hole cards. A card is burned before the flop (three shared cards), the turn (one) and the river (one)
- **Betting.** There is a round of betting before the flop and after each deal. Before the flop the player left of the big blind acts first; heads-up that is the button. After the flop the first player still in left of the button acts first. On your turn you fold, check (when you owe nothing), call, or bet or raise
- **Bets and raises.** A first bet is at least the big blind. A raise must add at least as much as the last bet or raise in the round. Nobody may bet more than they have. A player may go all in for less than that minimum; an all-in that is less than a full raise does not reopen the betting to players who have already acted, unless the raises since they acted add up to a full raise
- **End of a round.** A round ends when every player still in has acted and matched the highest bet, or is all in. When at most one player can still bet, the rest of the board is dealt with no more betting
- **Side pots.** A player who is all in can win only as much from each other player as they put in. The chips above that make side pots, each won only by the players who put chips into it. A bet nobody called goes back to the player who made it
- **Winning a hand.** If everyone else folds, the last player wins the pot without showing their cards. Otherwise the hands still in are shown, and each pot goes to the best five-card hand made from a player's two cards and the five shared cards (any five, including the board alone). The ranks, high to low: straight flush (a royal flush is the best), four of a kind, full house, flush, straight, three of a kind, two pair, one pair, high card. Kickers break ties; aces are high, and A-2-3-4-5 is the lowest straight. Suits never count
- **Split pots.** Tied hands split the pot equally. Odd chips go one each to the tied winners closest to the left of the button, going clockwise
- **Between hands.** The result shows each pot and who won it, and the next hand is dealt once every connected player with chips has pressed Continue
- **Running out.** A player with no chips is out for the rest of the game. They can watch but are not dealt in, skipped for the button and blinds, and do not vote
- **Ending.** Between hands any player with chips may vote to end the game. The game ends when every player still holding chips has voted yes, and the final screen ranks everyone by chips; the most chips wins (equal chips share the win). A vote may be taken back, and votes clear when the next hand starts or a voter leaves. The game also ends when one player has every chip
- **Hidden cards.** You see only your own hole cards. At a showdown the hands still in are shown to everyone with their best hand named. A player who folds, or who wins because everyone else folded, keeps their cards hidden. The deck order is hidden from everyone
- **Leaving.** A disconnected player keeps their seat and the game waits for them on their turn; the next hand does not wait for them. A dismissed player folds their hand and their chips leave the table; what they already bet stays in the pot and the hand goes on. If that leaves one player with chips, they win
- Any player can ask to undo the last move. It is undone only if every connected player approves, and the deck is reshuffled so an undo cannot reveal the next card. Continue and votes are not moves, so an undo goes back past them

## Controls

- On your turn click Fold, Check, or Call (the button shows what you owe)
- To bet or raise, type the total you want your bet to be this round in Amount and click Bet or Raise to; All in bets everything you have. The amount starts at the smallest raise allowed
- The table shows each player's chips, their bet this round, their cards (yours face up, everyone else's face down) and the D for the dealer button, with the current player outlined. The middle shows the board, the pot and each side pot with who can win it
- After a hand, read the result and click Continue. Click Vote to end the game to vote, and click again to take your vote back
- Click Undo to ask everyone to undo the last move
- Click FAQ at the top of the page for a short guide to the rules and controls

## Differences from Wikipedia

These follow no-limit play in [Wikipedia's Texas hold 'em article](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Texas_hold_%27em). What is different:

- **Fixed blinds.** Tournaments raise the blinds over time. Here they stay at 10 and 20, the owner's choice
- **Who has the first button.** The article does not say; here the first player to join has it
- **Moving the button.** The article moves the button one seat each hand and does not cover players who have run out of chips. Here it moves to the next player who still has chips, and the blinds follow it, with no dead button or missed blinds
- **Short all-ins.** The article says an all-in that is less than a full raise does not let the earlier raiser raise again. Here that applies to every player who has already acted, and the raises since they acted are added up, so several short all-ins that make a full raise do reopen the betting
- **Side pots.** The article does not describe side pots. They follow the usual casino rule described above
- **No mucking or showing by choice.** At a showdown every hand still in is shown at once, in no order, and a player who wins uncontested cannot choose to show
- **Running out of chips and ending.** The article does not cover the end of a cash game. Here a player with no chips is out, and the game ends by a unanimous vote of the players with chips or when one player has every chip, the owner's choices
- **Leaving.** The article does not cover a player leaving mid-hand. Here their hand is folded and their chips leave the table
- **Players.** This game seats 2 to 8, or up to 10 when the host ticks the override. With 10 players the deck still covers 20 hole cards, the burn cards and the five shared cards
