# Parchisi

Roll two dice and race your four pawns around the cross to home.

Players are seated in join order: Player 1 (blue) at the bottom, Player 2 (red) at the top, Player 3 (green) on the right, Player 4 (purple) on the left. With two players they sit on opposite sides. Turns pass to the player on the left, clockwise around the board: bottom, left, top, right. The player list shows the players in turn order.

## Rules

- Each player has four pawns in their nest. The track has 68 squares around the cross, 12 of them safe, and each color has a home column of 7 squares leading to the center
- Once two players are seated, each player rolls one die and the lowest roll goes first. Players tied for the lowest roll again
- The dice are rolled for you at the start of your turn. Move one pawn by each die, in either order, or one pawn by the total of both dice. A pawn moved by the total goes one die at a time, so where the first die lands must be a legal square too, and it captures there
- Every die that can be used must be. If only one die can be used, it must be the larger one when either could be. If no move is possible the turn passes on by itself with a message
- A 5 on either die, or two dice adding up to 5 (which uses both), moves a pawn from the nest onto your entry square
- Doubles give a move for each die. When all four of your pawns are out of the nest (home counts as out), doubles also give the two faces underneath (7 minus the top face), so four moves totalling 14, split among your pawns as you like. You roll again only if every die of the doubles was used. Three doubles in a row send your pawn closest to home, even one in your home column, back to the nest and end your turn
- Landing on a single opponent pawn on a square that is not safe captures it: it goes back to its nest and you earn a 20 square bonus move. You cannot land on a safe square held by an opponent, except that entering a pawn captures a single opponent standing on your entry square
- Getting a pawn home earns a 10 square bonus move. Bonuses are taken after all the dice, each used whole by one pawn already out of the nest. A bonus that no pawn can use is lost
- Two pawns of the same color on a square form a blockade that nobody can pass or land on, including their owner, and an opponent cannot enter onto it. A square never holds more than two pawns. On doubles, once one pawn leaves a blockade, the other stays there for the rest of that roll, so a blockade never moves forward together
- Reaching the center takes an exact roll. The first player with all four pawns home wins
- If a player leaves mid-game, their pawns are removed from the board and their turns are skipped. The last player left wins
- Any player can ask to undo the last move. It is undone only if every player still in the game approves

## Controls

- On your turn, the pawns you can move glow. Click one to select it, and every square it can reach is marked with the dice that get it there: 4 for one die, 2+3 for the total of both, or +20 for a bonus
- Click a marked square to move. Click the selected pawn again to unselect it
- The dice beside the board show the current roll; doubles with all pawns out also show the two faces underneath, with dashed edges. Used dice are faded and bonus moves appear next to them
- The board is dimmed when it is not your turn, and the last move's squares are outlined
- Click Undo to ask everyone to undo the last move; it happens only if every player still in the game approves
- Click FAQ at the top of the page for a short guide to the rules and controls

## Differences from Wikipedia

- Three doubles in a row are penalized. Wikipedia lists this as a variant ("some variations"); it is kept from the original version of this game, sending back the pawn closest to home as Wikipedia describes
- A blockade can only be broken by its owner. Wikipedia says another player may break a blockade with a roll of double one, but not how, so this is not implemented
- Local rules that limit how long a blockade may stay are not implemented
- A pawn always turns into its home column after one lap. Wikipedia says (without a citation) that a pawn may skip the home column and go around again
- The dice are rolled for you as soon as a turn starts, and the turn ends by itself once no move is left
