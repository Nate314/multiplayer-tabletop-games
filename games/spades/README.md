# Spades

Bid your tricks with your partner, spades are always trump, first team to the target score (500 unless the host chose another), for exactly 4 players.

## Rules

- **Teams.** Four players in two partnerships, partners sitting across: seats 1 and 3 against seats 2 and 4
- **Deal.** The whole 52-card deck is dealt, 13 cards each. Seat 4 deals the first hand and the deal passes to the left after each hand
- **Bidding.** Starting left of the dealer and ending with the dealer, each player bids once: 1 to 13 tricks, or nil (0) to take none. A team's contract is its two bids added up, a nil adding nothing. Every bid is shown to everyone
- **Tricks.** The player left of the dealer leads the first trick, and the winner of each trick leads the next. Follow the suit led if you can; otherwise play any card. The highest spade takes the trick, else the highest card of the suit led. Aces are high
- **Breaking spades.** A spade may not be led until a spade has been played in an earlier trick, unless the leader holds nothing but spades
- **Contract.** A team that takes at least its contract scores 10 points per trick bid and 1 point per extra trick (a bag). A team that takes fewer loses 10 points per trick bid. Both partners' tricks count toward the contract, a nil bidder's included. Scores can go below zero
- **Bags.** Bags add up from hand to hand. When a team reaches 10 it loses 100 points and its count drops by 10, so the rest carry on
- **Nil.** A nil bidder who takes no trick scores 100 for the team; one who takes any trick loses 100. The nil is scored on its own, apart from the partner's contract
- **Scoring screen.** After each hand a scoring screen shows each team's contract, nils, bags and totals, and the next hand is dealt once every connected player has pressed Continue (disconnected players are not waited for)
- **Winning.** The first team to reach the target score (500 unless the host chose 250 or 300) wins, and a team that falls to -200 loses, whatever the target. If both teams pass the target in the same hand the higher total wins; if the totals are equal another hand is played
- You see only your own hand and how many cards everyone else holds. Bids and the tricks each player has taken are shown
- **Leaving.** A disconnected player keeps their seat and the game waits for them on their turn. A dismissed player breaks their partnership, so the game ends and the other team wins
- Any player can ask to undo the last move. It is undone only if every connected player approves, and only while all four seats are filled. Continue is not a move, so an undo goes back past it

## Controls

- When it is your turn to bid, choose a number of tricks (or Nil) and click Bid
- In a trick, the cards you may play are raised and outlined; click one to play it
- The table shows both teams' scores and bags, each team's contract and tricks once the bidding is done, the hand number, whether spades are broken, and the four seats with you at the bottom and your partner across. Each seat shows its card count, the dealer, its bid and the tricks it has taken. The middle shows the trick being played, or the last trick taken with who took it
- After each hand, read the scoring screen and click Continue; after the last hand click Close, and Show Scoring opens it again
- Click Undo to ask everyone to undo the last move
- Click Rules at the top of the page for these rules, and FAQ in it for a short guide to the rules and controls

## Differences from Wikipedia

These follow the main rules in [Wikipedia's Spades article](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spades_(card_game)). What is different:

- **Breaking spades is always on.** The article lists it as a common rule rather than part of the basic game. Here it is always played, as the site's design asks
- **No blind nil.** The article describes bidding blind nil before looking at the cards. That is not offered here, and neither are the other bidding variants it lists
- **Dealing.** The article draws for the first dealer and deals one card at a time. Here seat 4 deals first and the shuffled deck is split straight into four hands, which gives the same chances. The deal passes to the left as in the article
- **Both partners nil.** The article does not say how a team with two nil bids scores. Here there is no contract, so the team scores only its two nils and takes no bags
- **Target score.** The host can choose 250, 300 or 500 before play starts, and everyone must agree. The -200 loss does not change with it
- **Both teams past the target.** The article does not cover both teams passing the target, or one passing it while the other falls to -200, in the same hand. Here the higher total wins; on an equal total another hand is played, as the article does for a tie
- **Reneging cannot happen.** The article has penalties for not following suit when able. Here a card that does not follow suit when it must is refused, so there is no penalty
- **Reviewing tricks.** The last trick taken stays in the middle until the next card is played, and the tricks each player has taken are shown
- **Leaving.** The rules do not cover a player leaving mid-game. Here the partnership is broken, so the game ends and the other team wins
