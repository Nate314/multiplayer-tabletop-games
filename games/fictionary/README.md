# Fictionary

Write a believable fake definition of a rare word, then spot the real one among everyone's fakes, for 3 to 8 players. The game plays 8 words, and the highest score wins.

## Rules

- **Words.** Each round everyone sees the same rare word, shown in large type with the round number. The game holds the real definition. Its 245 words and their definitions were checked against Wiktionary and Dictionary.com, and the definitions are written in this game's own words
- **Writing.** Every player writes a fake definition that sounds real, up to 150 characters, and can change it until writing ends. Nobody else sees what you wrote until the reveal; the others see only that you have written
- **Writing time.** Writing lasts 2 minutes, and ends early once everyone has written. When the time runs out, whoever has not written has no entry that round
- **Writing the real definition.** A definition that holds the real one as whole words, ignoring capitals and punctuation, or is one letter away from it, scores 3 points. It is not shown as a choice, and its writer does not vote that round, since they know the answer. On the reveal the real definition reads "Real definition, also written by" and their name
- **Equal fakes.** Fakes that are the same once capitals, spaces and punctuation are ignored are shown once, as one choice credited to every author. Every fake is shown in lowercase without a closing period, as the real definitions are
- **Voting.** Once writing ends, the fakes and the real definition are shown in a shuffled list with no names, and every other player votes for the one they think is real. Your own fake is marked Yours and cannot be picked. You can change your vote until voting ends. Voting lasts 60 seconds, and ends early once everyone has voted
- **Skipped vote.** When no voter can be fooled, the vote is skipped and the reveal follows at once. This happens whenever every voter wrote every fake: when nobody wrote a fake, when everyone wrote the real definition, or when the only voters are the authors of the only fake
- **Scoring.** Voting for the real definition scores 2 points. Each vote for a fake scores 1 point for each of its authors. Writing the real definition scores 3 points
- **Reveal.** The reveal shows which definition is real, who wrote each fake, who voted for each one and everyone's points for the word. Everyone clicks Continue to start the next word; disconnected players are not waited for
- **Winning.** After the last word the highest score wins. Players with equal scores share the win
- **House rules.** The host can set the number of words (5, 8 or 10) and the writing time (1 minute, 90 seconds, 2 minutes or 3 minutes). Everyone must agree to them before play starts
- **Hidden.** Until the reveal, no player's browser is told which choice is real, who wrote the other choices or how anyone else voted. History and a table screen show only the choice texts
- **Leaving.** A player who leaves keeps their seat and can rejoin. A dismissed player's definition and vote are removed, though a fake already shown stays a choice. If that completes the writing or the voting, the phase ends at once. If fewer than 3 players are left, the game ends at once with the scores as they stand
- **No undo.** There are no turns to take back: change your definition or your vote while the phase is open instead

## Controls

- Type your fake in the box labelled Your definition and click Submit. To change it, type a new one and click Update
- Click the definition you think is real. Click another one to change your vote
- After each reveal, click Continue
- Click Rules at the top of the page for these rules, and FAQ in it for a short guide to the rules and controls

## Differences from Wikipedia

These follow [Wikipedia's Fictionary article](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fictionary), played online. What is different:

- **No picker.** In the article one player, the picker, chooses a word from a dictionary, collects the definitions, reads them out and does not vote, and the dictionary then passes on. Here the game picks the word and holds the real definition, so everyone writes and votes every round
- **On screen.** The article's definitions are written on paper, read aloud twice and voted on in turn. Here they are typed, shown on screen and voted on at once, with a writing time (1 minute, 90 seconds, 2 minutes or 3 minutes; 2 minutes unless the host chose another) and a 60 second vote, each ending early once everyone is in
- **Scoring.** The article scores 1 point for a correct vote, 1 for each vote your definition gets, and 3 to the picker when nobody finds the real one. Here a correct vote scores 2, a vote for your fake 1, and writing the real definition 3 (the game matches it, allowing one letter of slack). There are no picker points
- **Writing the real definition.** The article has the picker choose another word when a player knows it. Here a player who writes the real definition scores 3, their entry is not shown and they do not vote. Equal fakes are shown once and credited to every author, and a vote nobody can lose is skipped
- **Own votes.** The article mentions a variant that allows voting for your own definition. Here your own fake can never be picked
- **Fixed length.** The article plays rounds of the dictionary going around and does not say when the game ends. Here the game plays a fixed number of words (8 unless the host chose 5 or 10), and the highest score wins
- **Leaving.** The article does not cover a player leaving. Here a dismissed player's entry and vote are removed, and the game ends with the scores as they stand if fewer than 3 players remain. There is no undo
