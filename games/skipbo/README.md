# Skip-Bo

Build piles from 1 to 12 with your hand, your stock and your discard piles, and be the first to empty your stock, for 2 to 6 players.

## Rules

- The 162-card deck: twelve each of the numbers 1 to 12 and 18 Skip-Bo cards, which are wild
- **Setup.** Each player is dealt a stock pile of 30 cards with 2 to 4 players, or 20 with 5 or 6. Only the top card of each stock is face up. The rest of the deck is the face-down draw pile. Player 1 goes first, and play goes up through the seats
- **Your turn.** When your turn starts, you draw from the draw pile until you hold 5 cards. Then you may play any number of cards onto the four shared building piles: from your hand, the top of your stock, or the top of any of your four discard piles. If you play every card in your hand, you draw 5 more and go on. End your turn by putting one card from your hand on one of your discard piles
- **Building piles.** An empty building pile starts with a 1 or a Skip-Bo. Each pile then takes the next number up, to 12. A Skip-Bo counts as the number the pile needs next. A pile that reaches 12 is shuffled into the draw pile, and its space is free for a new pile
- **Discard piles.** Each player has four. Any hand card may go on any of your own discard piles, in any order, but only as the discard that ends your turn. Only the top card of a discard pile can be played, and only onto a building pile, never onto another discard pile. Every card in every discard pile is shown to everyone, as the piles are fanned at a table
- **Winning.** The first player to play the last card of their stock wins at once. It is a single game with no points
- **Running out.** If the draw pile is empty, you draw what is left, or nothing. A player with no cards in hand and nothing to draw cannot discard, so they end their turn with End turn after playing what they can. If every player in a row ends their turn that way, nobody can ever play again, and the players with the fewest stock cards left win
- You see your own hand, the top card and size of every stock, every discard pile and the building piles. Other hands, the cards under each stock top and the draw pile are hidden
- A disconnected player keeps their seat and their turn waits for them. A dismissed player's hand, stock and discard piles are shuffled into the draw pile and their turns are skipped. If it was their turn, the next player's turn starts. The last player left wins
- Any player can ask to undo the last move. It is undone only if every player still in the game approves

## Controls

- On your turn, click a card in your hand, your stock top or the top card of one of your discard piles to pick it. The building piles it fits are outlined; click one to play the card there
- With a hand card picked, your four discard piles are outlined too; click one to discard the card there and end your turn
- Click the picked card again, or Put back, to pick something else
- With no cards in your hand and none to draw, click End turn
- Each building pile shows its top card (a Skip-Bo shows the number it stands for) and the number it needs next. The draw pile shows how many cards are left
- Every player is shown with their hand size, stock size and stock top, and their four discard piles. The player whose turn it is is outlined, and the winner is outlined at the end
- Click Undo to ask everyone to undo the last move
- Click FAQ at the top of the page for a short guide to the rules and controls

## Differences from Wikipedia

These follow the rules described on [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Skip-Bo), and Mattel's rules where the article is silent. What remains different:

- **Who goes first.** Wikipedia has the player of middle age go first. Here Player 1, the first to join, goes first
- **Drawing the first hand.** Each player draws their 5 cards as their own first turn starts, as Mattel's rules have it, so other players hold no cards before their first turn
- **Completed piles.** Wikipedia says only that a pile that reaches 12 is removed. Here it is shuffled into the draw pile at once, so the draw pile is refilled as the game goes
- **Partnerships and points.** Wikipedia describes partnerships for six or more players and a play-to-500 points game. Here every player plays alone, with 2 to 6 players, and the game is a single round
- **Nobody can play.** The rules do not cover every player being out of cards with nothing left to draw and nothing to play. Here, once every player in a row has ended a turn that way, the fewest stock cards left win, and tied players share the win
- **Leaving.** The rules do not cover a player leaving mid-game. Here their cards are shuffled into the draw pile
