# Monopoly

Buy, trade and build on properties, charge rent, and bankrupt every other player, for 2 to 8 players.

## Rules

- **Board and cards.** The standard US board with the current prices, rents, house costs and mortgage values, and the current 16 Chance and 16 Community Chest cards. Both decks are shuffled at the start; a drawn card goes to the bottom of its deck, except a Get Out of Jail Free card, which the player keeps until it is used, then it goes back under its deck
- **Start.** Everyone has $1500 on GO. Everyone rolls both dice and the highest total goes first; players tied for highest roll again. Turns then go up through the seats
- **Moving.** Roll two dice and move by the total. Passing or landing on GO collects $200. Doubles roll again after the move; a third doubles in a row goes straight to Jail without moving
- **Buying and auctions.** Landing on a property the bank owns, you may buy it at its price. If you decline, it is auctioned to every player still in the game, you included. Bids are whole dollars, any amount above the high bid and up to the bidder's cash. A pass is final; when everyone but the high bidder has passed, they buy at their bid. If nobody bids, the bank keeps it
- **Rent.** Paid at once on landing. A street's rent is doubled while its owner has the whole color group and it has no buildings, even when another street of the group is mortgaged. Houses and hotels charge the rent on the Title Deed. Railroads charge $25, $50, $100 or $200 for one to four owned, utilities 4 times the dice for one and 10 times for both. Mortgaged property charges nothing. Owners in Jail still collect
- **Building.** On your turn, with a whole color group and none of it mortgaged, buy houses at the group's house cost. Build evenly: no street may have more than one building more than another of its group. A hotel takes four houses on every street of the group, and gives those four houses back to the bank. The bank has 32 houses and 12 hotels; when they run out, nobody can build. Buildings sell back for half their cost, evenly; a hotel sells back as four houses when the bank has them, and outright for half its whole cost (five times the house cost) when it does not
- **Mortgages.** A property with no buildings in its color group mortgages for half its price. Lifting the mortgage costs the mortgage value plus 10%, rounded up to a dollar
- **Cards.** Chance: advance to Boardwalk, GO, Illinois Avenue, St. Charles Place or Reading Railroad (collecting $200 for passing GO); advance to the nearest railroad (two cards; owned, pay twice the rent) or utility (owned, roll the dice and pay ten times the roll); a $50 dividend; Get Out of Jail Free; go back 3 spaces; Go to Jail; general repairs ($25 a house, $100 a hotel); a $15 speeding fine; pay each player $50; collect $150. Community Chest: advance to GO; collect $200, $100 (three cards), $50, $25, $20 or $10; pay $100 or $50 (two cards); Get Out of Jail Free; Go to Jail; collect $10 from every player; street repairs ($40 a house, $115 a hotel)
- **Taxes.** Income Tax $200 and Luxury Tax $100, paid to the bank. Free Parking is just a resting place: no money is collected there
- **Jail.** You go to Jail by landing on Go to Jail, drawing a Go to Jail card or rolling a third doubles, and you collect nothing for passing GO. On a turn in Jail you may first pay $50 or use a Get Out of Jail Free card and then roll as usual, or roll for doubles. Doubles get you out and move you, with no extra roll. After a third failed roll you pay $50 and move by that roll. In Jail you still collect rent, build, trade and bid
- **Trading.** Any player may offer any other player a trade at any time: properties with no buildings in their color group, cash and Get Out of Jail Free cards, on either side. One offer is open at a time, and nothing moves until the other player accepts. An offer is refused if either player could not pay the 10% interest on the mortgaged property they would receive. An offer from or to a player who disconnects is dropped
- **Receiving mortgaged property.** Whoever receives a mortgaged property, in a trade or a bankruptcy, decides at once for each one: lift the mortgage for its value plus 10%, or pay the 10% now and keep it mortgaged (lifting it later costs the value plus 10% again). The game waits for that choice
- **Paying debts.** A payment you cannot make waits as a debt, and the game waits for you: sell buildings, mortgage or trade, and the debt is paid as soon as you have the cash. A card that has several players pay you (your birthday) waits on each of them in turn
- **Bankruptcy.** If you cannot raise what you owe, declare bankruptcy. Bankrupt to a player, your buildings are sold to the bank at half price and that player takes all your cash, properties and Get Out of Jail Free cards, and decides about each mortgaged property as above. Bankrupt to the bank, your buildings go back to the bank, your jail cards under their decks, and each of your properties is auctioned, unmortgaged, to the players left. On your turn you may also declare bankruptcy without a debt, to the bank, to leave the game
- **Winning.** The last player who is not bankrupt wins
- Nothing is hidden: every player's cash, properties and cards are shown. Only the order of the two decks is secret
- A dismissed player is out as if bankrupt: to the player they owe money, if any, who takes everything; otherwise to the bank, and their properties are auctioned to the others. Any trade offer from or to them and any other debt they owe or are owed is dropped. If it was their turn, the next player's turn begins. The last player left wins
- Any player can ask to undo the last move. It is undone only if every player still in the game approves. Bids, trades and their answers are not moves, so an undo goes back past them too, and a roll played again may come up differently

## Controls

- The side panel shows what you can do now: Roll the dice (or Roll again after doubles), Buy or Decline and auction, Pay $50, Use jail card or Roll for doubles in Jail, and End turn. While you owe money it says how much and to whom
- Click a property on the board, or its chip in the player list, to open its Title Deed. On your turn the deed has Build house (or Build hotel), Sell building, Mortgage and Lift mortgage for your own properties; a greyed out button explains why when you hover over it. While you owe money only selling and mortgaging work
- During an auction every player still in the game sees the auction panel: quick bids of $1, $10 or $50 over the high bid, a box for any amount, Bid and Pass
- Click Propose a trade, choose a player, tick what each side gives and set the cash and jail cards, then Send offer. The player it is for sees Accept and Decline; you can Withdraw it until then
- Declare bankruptcy asks you to confirm first
- The board shows each space's owner (or price), houses as green squares, a hotel as a red bar, mortgaged property, and every token, with jailed tokens in a dashed box on the Jail space. The middle shows the last roll and the last card drawn. The player list shows everyone's cash, properties and jail cards, and Recent events lists what happened, newest first
- Click Undo to ask everyone to undo the last move
- Click FAQ at the top of the page for a short guide to the rules and controls

## Differences from Wikipedia

These follow the rules described on [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monopoly_(game)) and, where it is silent, the current official Hasbro rules. What remains different:

- **Rent is collected for the owner.** At a table the owner must ask for rent before the next roll or it is lost. Here rent is always paid on landing
- **Auctions.** Wikipedia gives no auction procedure. Here bids are limited to the cash a player holds, a pass is final, a disconnected player counts as passed unless they hold the high bid, and an auction with no bids leaves the property with the bank. While an auction runs, the player who must act can only sell buildings, mortgage or declare bankruptcy, and everyone can still trade
- **No house auctions.** When several players want the last houses, the rules auction them. Here houses go to whoever builds first
- **Building and mortgaging off-turn.** The rules allow building and mortgaging between other players' turns. Here they are moves made on your own turn, or while you owe money (selling and mortgaging only)
- **Selling a hotel when the bank is short of houses** is not covered by Wikipedia. Here the hotel then sells outright for half its whole cost
- **One open trade at a time.** At a table several deals can be talked over at once. Here one offer waits for its answer before another can be made
- **Debts.** The rules do not say what happens when several payments are owed at once. Here they are paid in order, and a player bankrupt to one creditor gives everything to that creditor; their other debts are dropped
- **Bankruptcy without a debt.** A player may declare bankruptcy to the bank on their turn to leave the game, as a resignation
- **The third failed roll in Jail** always pays the $50 fine, even for a player holding a Get Out of Jail Free card
- **Leaving.** The rules do not cover a player leaving. Here a dismissed player is out as if bankrupt, to the player they owe or else to the bank, and a trade offer from or to a player who disconnects is dropped
