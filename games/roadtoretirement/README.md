# Road to Retirement

Choose college or work, build a career and a family, and retire the richest, for 2 to 6 players. The host can switch to the 1860 Checkered board, a race across a checkerboard of named squares to Happy Old Age.

It plays like The Game of Life, on an original road with original careers, houses and events.

## Rules

### The Road to Retirement board

- **The road.** A winding road of spaces from Start to Retirement, with two forks. At Start every player chooses College or Straight to work; halfway along, the road forks into the Safe road and the Fast lane, which is shorter and full of risk spaces. Both branches of each fork join again
- **Start.** Everyone starts on Start with $25,000 (the host's Starting cash can change it). Seat 1 goes first and turns go around in seat order, skipping anyone who has retired
- **College or Straight to work.** On your first turn you choose. College costs $50,000 and leads along the college road to Graduation, where you pick one of two careers that need a degree. Straight to work offers at once two careers that need no degree, and the work road is shorter. Either way you then spin on the same turn
- **Spinning.** Spin 1 to 10 and move that many spaces. Every payday you pass or land on pays your career's salary. The space you land on then acts
- **Stop spaces.** Graduation, the Wedding, the house stop and Retirement stop a spin that reaches them, and the rest of the spin is lost. At the fork the move pauses while you choose the Safe road or the Fast lane, then goes on with the steps left
- **Spaces.** A gain or a loss pays or costs the amount shown. A job offers two careers you can take (any career with a degree, or one that needs no degree without), and you pick one or keep yours. A house space or the house stop offers two houses nobody owns yet; buy one or take No house. A family space or the Wedding brings a partner if you have none; each family space after that brings a child. A risk space spins again: 1 to 4 loses $20,000, 5 to 7 breaks even, and 8 to 10 wins $50,000
- **Loans.** Whenever money would go below zero you take loans of $20,000, as many as it takes. Each loan costs $25,000 to repay at retirement
- **Retirement.** Reaching Retirement ends your road. Your worth is your cash, plus what your houses sell for (each sells for more than it cost), plus $25,000 for each child, minus $25,000 for each loan, so it can be below zero
- **Winning.** Once everyone has retired, the highest worth wins; players who tie share the win
- **House rules.** Board picks Road to Retirement (the standard) or 1860 Checkered. Game length Short leaves out the dimmed spaces, so the road is about 30 spaces shorter; Full (the standard) plays them all. Starting cash is $10,000, $25,000 (the standard) or $50,000. Game length and Starting cash are offered only with the Road to Retirement board

### The 1860 Checkered board

- **The board.** An 8 by 8 checkerboard; pieces stand on the 32 light squares, every one of them named. Everyone starts on Infancy in the bottom-left corner; the goal is Happy Old Age in the top-right corner, worth 50 points
- **The teetotum.** Spin 1 to 6. 1 moves up or down to the next light square in the same column, 2 left or right to the next light square in the same row, 3 one square diagonally. 4, 5 and 6 move the same ways one or two squares. You choose among the squares the spin allows; with one, you move there at once
- **Named squares.** A square with an arrow sends you on to its target: Bravery to Honor, School to College, Honesty to Happiness, Industry to Wealth, Ambition to Fame, Perseverance to Success, Politics to Congress, Cupid to Matrimony, Influence to Fat Office, Gambling to Ruin, Idleness to Disgrace, Intemperance to Poverty and Crime to Prison. The target acts but never sends you again. Wealth scores 10 points; Honor, Happiness, College, Success, Fat Office and Congress score 5 each, also when you are sent there
- **Prison** loses your next turn. **Despair** sends you back to Infancy with every point lost. **Speculation** spins again: 3 or 6 goes on to Wealth, anything else to Ruin. Truth, Matrimony, Fame, Ruin, Poverty, Disgrace and Jail do nothing
- **Winning.** Land on Happy Old Age with at least 100 points counting its 50, and you win. Arriving short, you keep playing and collect more; the 50 is not kept
- Turns go around in seat order

### Both boards

- Nothing is hidden: everyone sees every player's money, career, houses and family, or points
- **Leaving.** A disconnected player keeps their seat and the game waits for them on their turn. A dismissed player's token leaves the board and their turns are skipped; if it was their turn, the next player starts a fresh turn. On the road the game ends once every player left has retired
- Any player can ask to undo the last move. It is undone only if every player still in the game approves. A spin played again after an undo spins again, so it may land somewhere else

## Controls

- Click Spin to spin the spinner (1 to 10) or the teetotum (1 to 6). The number spun shows on the spinner, and the status line beside or below the board says what happened
- On your first turn on the road, click College or Straight to work. At the fork, click Safe road or Fast lane
- When careers are offered, click one (its salary is shown), or Keep with your current career. When houses are offered, click one (its price is shown) or No house
- The legend under the road names each space color; stop spaces have a thick border, and the spaces left out of a Short game are dimmed. With a mouse, hover over a space to read what it does
- On the 1860 board the squares the spin allows are highlighted; click one to move there
- The Players list shows each player's cash, loans, career and salary, degree, houses and family on the road, or points on the 1860 board, and marks the winner, a player who left and a player who loses their next turn
- Click Undo to ask everyone to undo the last move
- Open the ☰ menu and choose History to step through every move, with the mover's space or square outlined
- Open the ☰ menu and choose Rules for these rules, and FAQ in it for a short guide to the rules and controls

## Differences from Wikipedia

These follow the rules in [Wikipedia's The Game of Life article](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Game_of_Life), which describes the modern game only in outline. What is different:

- **The same.** A spinner of 1 to 10 moves the players, and the player who retires with the most money wins
- **An original board.** The road, the careers, the houses and the event texts are this game's own. Nothing on the board is copied from the commercial game
- **Left out.** There are no cars, pegs, bills, insurance policies, stock certificates, Life Tiles or Day of Reckoning, and no spaces that move a player backward. Money is a number in the Players list, and a partner and children are listed by the player's name
- **The numbers.** The article gives no prices. Here College costs $50,000, a loan is $20,000 and costs $25,000 to repay at retirement, each child is worth $25,000 at retirement, and a risk spin of 1 to 4 loses $20,000, 5 to 7 breaks even and 8 to 10 wins $50,000. Careers pay $55,000 to $100,000 with a degree and $20,000 to $50,000 without; houses cost $40,000 to $300,000
- **Paydays.** A payday pays your salary whether you pass it or land on it, with no bonus for landing on it
- **Stop spaces.** Graduation, the Wedding, the house stop and Retirement stop a spin, and the fork pauses it for a choice
- **Loans.** The article's $20,000 promissory notes are taken by themselves whenever money would go below zero, as many as needed, and repaid at $25,000 each at retirement, which can leave a worth below zero
- **Houses.** A house sells at retirement for more than it cost, and two houses nobody owns are offered on each house space
- **Ties.** The article does not cover equal worths; here they share the win
- **House rules.** Board, Game length and Starting cash are this game's own (see Rules)
- **The 1860 board.** The article says landing on Suicide eliminated the player. Here that square is named Despair and sends the player back to Infancy with every point lost, so nobody leaves the game by it, and the article's rule that the last player left alive wins is not needed. The teetotum's moves are counted in light squares (see The 1860 board's sources). Arriving at Happy Old Age short of 100 points, the 50 is not kept. A square you are sent to never sends you again

## The 1860 board's sources

- **The scan.** The squares are copied from [File:CheckeredGameofLife.jpg](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:CheckeredGameofLife.jpg) on Wikimedia Commons, public domain in the US, a scan of The Checkered Game of Life from George Mason University's Center for History and New Media. It was checked against [File:Checkered game of life board 1866.jpg](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Checkered_game_of_life_board_1866.jpg), the printing patented March 30th 1866, which is the same except that its Speculation square reads "Government Contract to Wealth". The Smithsonian's copy (object nmah_321475) has no open image and is dated after 1910, so it was not used
- **What could not be read.** Both scans show only the board: no printed rules sheet for The Checkered Game of Life was found. Every square on the board could be read, but the rules come from the secondary sources, Wikipedia and thebiggamehunter.com. baldwin-games.com was also to be checked, but its page for this game could not be reached (HTTP 404). The squares with no points or send printed on the board (Truth, Matrimony, Fame, Ruin, Poverty, Disgrace and Jail) do nothing
- **Speculation.** thebiggamehunter.com says that in 1860 Speculation went on to Wealth on a 3 or a 6 and to Ruin otherwise, and that later printings made it Government Contract to Wealth. This game plays the 1860 rule, as the scan used is the 1860 board
- **Suicide.** Wikipedia and thebiggamehunter.com both say landing on it removed the player. Here it is renamed Despair and sends the player back to Infancy with every point lost, as this game's design chose
- **The teetotum's moves.** thebiggamehunter.com gives 1 or 4 up or down, 2 or 5 left or right, 3 or 6 diagonally, one or two spaces. Since a straight move of one square on a checkerboard lands on a dark square, moves here are counted in light squares: up, down, left and right skip the dark square between
- **Occupied squares.** thebiggamehunter.com says landing on a square another player is on sends that player to Jail. That rule is left out: pieces share squares, and Jail is a plain square
- **Happy Old Age.** thebiggamehunter.com says leaving Happy Old Age and coming back scores its 50 again. Here the 50 counts only for the win check on arrival and is not kept when short, so arriving short twice does not win by repetition
- **Last player left.** Wikipedia says the last player left alive wins. Not needed here, since nobody leaves the game by Despair
