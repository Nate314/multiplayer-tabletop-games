# Dominoes

Match tile ends to build a line that branches at doubles (or a single line, by house rule), draw from the boneyard when you are stuck, and be first to play out your hand, first to 100 points (61 with three or four players), for 2 to 4 players. This is the Draw game, the blocking game where you draw when you cannot play.

## Rules

- **Set.** A house rule: the host chooses double-six (28 tiles) or double-nine (55 tiles) in the house rules, before the first move. Everyone sees the choice and the tiles are dealt again. The first move locks it, with the other house rules
- **Doubles open branches.** A house rule with three settings, every double by default:
  - **None (a single line):** a double is played like any other tile, see "The line" below
  - **Starting double only:** the starting double opens four ends, one on each side. Later doubles are played like any other tile, and the line goes on from them. With starting double only, if the round is not led by a double, the first double played opens the branches: the end it is played on goes on past it, and two more ends branch from its sides
  - **Every double:** every double opens branches. The starting double opens four ends; a double played on an end opens three, the end going on past it and one on each side. Each new end takes the double's number
- **Deal.** Each player is dealt 7 tiles from a double-six set (6 with four players) or 9 from a double-nine set. The rest form the boneyard, hidden from everyone
- **First lead.** In the first round the holder of the highest double must lead with it. If nobody holds a double, the heaviest tile (most pips, then the higher end) leads. In later rounds the winner of the previous round leads with any tile; after a tied round the first-round rule applies again
- **The line.** With Doubles open branches set to None, tiles are played in one line with two open ends, the left and the right. There are no branches: a double is played like any other tile. A tile must match the number on the end it is played on, and it is flipped so the matching half touches the line. A tile that fits both ends can go on either
- **Drawing.** A player with no tile that fits must draw from the boneyard, one tile at a time, until they can play or the boneyard is empty. You may not draw while you hold a tile that fits. The boneyard can be drawn empty
- **Passing.** A player with no tile that fits when the boneyard is empty passes
- **Going out.** A round ends when a player plays their last tile. They score the pips left in every other hand plus the pips in the boneyard
- **Blocked.** A round also ends the moment nobody can play and the boneyard is empty. The player with the fewest pips left wins the round and scores the pips in the other hands. If two or more players tie for the fewest, nobody scores
- **Winning.** The first player to reach 100 points wins with two players, or 61 with three or four. A scoring screen shows each round, and the next round is dealt once every connected player has pressed Continue (disconnected players are not waited for)
- You see only your own hand, how many tiles each other player holds and how many tiles are in the boneyard. Which tiles are in the boneyard is hidden from everyone
- **Leaving.** A disconnected player keeps their seat and the game waits for them on their turn. A dismissed player's tiles go back into the boneyard and their turns are skipped. If only one player is left, they win
- Any player can ask to undo the last move. It is undone only if every player still in the game approves, and the boneyard is shuffled again so an undone draw cannot show the next tile. Continue is not a move, so an undo goes back past it too

## Controls

- Playable tiles in your hand are raised and outlined; click one to play it. If it fits more than one end, buttons ask which end (on a single line, the Left end or the Right end), and Cancel puts it back
- Click Draw, or the boneyard, to draw when nothing fits. Click Pass when nothing fits and the boneyard is empty
- The table shows the round, every player's tile count and score in seat order, the boneyard, the open ends and the line of tiles, which wraps onto more rows when it is long. The player whose turn it is is outlined
- After each round, read the scoring screen and click Continue; after the last round click Close, and Show Scoring opens it again
- Click Undo to ask everyone to undo the last move
- Click FAQ at the top of the page for a short guide to the rules and controls

## Differences from Wikipedia

These follow the Draw game and Blocking game in the [Dominoes article](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dominoes) (sections "Draw game", "Blocking game", "Scoring"). What remains different:

- **Hand sizes.** The article gives no hand sizes for the Draw game. They follow [Pagat's Draw Dominoes](https://www.pagat.com/domino/draw.html), so a boneyard is left to draw from: 7 tiles with double-six for 2 or 3 players, 6 for 4 players, and 9 with double-nine for 2 to 4 players (Pagat does not cover double-nine, so 9 is this game's choice)
- **First lead.** The article says only that the holder of the double-six starts the Middle Eastern version. Here the highest double in the chosen set leads, and the heaviest tile leads when nobody holds a double. The first player must lead with that tile; the article does not say so
- **Drawing.** The article lets a player draw as many tiles as they like before playing, and says most rules keep two tiles in the boneyard. Here you may draw only while nothing fits, you must draw until you can play, and the boneyard can be drawn empty
- **Spinners.** The article's "Line of play" section says that in many games doubles are spinners, played on all four sides so the line branches, and that sometimes the first tile must be a double that serves as the only spinner. Doubles open branches follows this: Every double makes each double a spinner, Starting double only makes one double the only spinner, and None plays the single line. The article does not say what happens when the first tile is not a double: here, with Starting double only, the first double played becomes the spinner. That, and every double as the default, are this game's choices
- **Scoring.** The article scores the pips in the losing hands plus the stock. Here a player who goes out scores the other hands plus the boneyard, as the article says, and a blocked round scores the other hands only (the boneyard is empty then)
- **Blocked rounds.** The article awards a blocked round to the lightest hand, and gives no rule for a tie. Here a tie for the lightest hand scores nothing, and the first-round lead rule applies again in the next round
- **Target score.** The article gives no target for the Draw game. Following Pagat's Draw Dominoes, the game is played to 100 points for 2 players and 61 for 3 or 4 players (the target follows the players still seated)
- **Players.** The article describes two players, with adaptations for more. Here 2 to 4 play individually, with no teams
- **Leaving.** The rules do not cover a player leaving mid-game. Here their tiles go back into the boneyard, and if they held the tile that had to lead, the opening tile is chosen again among the hands left
