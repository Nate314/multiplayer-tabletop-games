# Whodunit

Move through a seaside hotel, make suggestions and be first to name who did it, with what and where, for 3 to 6 players.

## Rules

- **The cards.** 21 cards: 6 suspects, 6 objects and 9 rooms. At the start one suspect, one object and one room are picked at random and set aside face down: the answer. The other 18 cards are dealt out to the players, one at a time starting with seat 1, so with 4 or 5 players some hands are a card larger
- **Seats and tokens.** Play goes around in seat order, seat 1 first, and each seat plays its own suspect: seat 1 plays Commodore Brine, then Madame Coral, Doctor Tidewell, Miss Gull, Professor Kelp and Baroness Pearl. Tokens that no seat plays still stand on the board and can be named in suggestions. Each object starts in a different room, picked at random
- **The hotel.** Nine rooms in three rows of three, joined by corridors with a ring of corridor around the outside. A room is entered through one of its doors. Two pairs of corner rooms are joined by a secret passage: Boathouse and Wine Cellar, Sun Terrace and Galley
- **Your turn.** At the start you may roll two dice and move, take a secret passage if you are in a room that has one, stay in a room a suggestion moved you into, or accuse. After moving into a room you may make one suggestion. You may end your turn after moving, staying or suggesting
- **Moving.** Move exactly the total rolled along the corridors, one square at a time, never onto a square holding another token and never over the same square twice. A move that reaches a door with squares still to spare may go into that room and end there. A room you started the turn in, or left by taking a door on this move, cannot be entered again this move. If no square or room can be reached with the roll, you stay put
- **Secret passage.** Instead of rolling, a player in a corner room with a passage moves to the room at its other end, and may then make a suggestion
- **Suggesting.** In a room, name a suspect and an object. The room is the one you are in. That suspect's token and that object move into the room. Then the other players, in seat order after you, are asked in turn: the first who holds at least one of the three named cards shows you one of them. A player with one matching card shows it at once, and a player with several chooses which. Only you and the player who showed it see the card; everyone else sees only who showed a card, and which players could not show. If nobody holds any, nobody shows anything
- **Moved by a suggestion.** A suspect named in a suggestion is moved into the room. When that suspect's player next has a turn, they may stay in that room and make a suggestion there without rolling, once. If they roll instead, they move as usual
- **Accusing.** At the start of your turn, or after you have moved, name a suspect, an object and a room and confirm. If all three are the answer you win and the answer is shown to everyone. If not, you are out of turns for the rest of the game, but you still show cards when a suggestion asks you to. Your token stays where it is
- **Winning.** The first right accusation wins. If every player but one has accused wrongly, the one who can still accuse wins at once. If every player has accused wrongly, nobody wins and the answer is shown
- **Notes.** The game keeps a notes sheet for each player: every card listed with a mark you click to cycle through blank, yes, no and maybe. Only you see your notes. Your own cards and any cards face up are labeled on it
- **Hidden cards.** You see your own hand and how many cards everyone else holds. The answer stays hidden until the game ends. The History and the table screen show no hand and no shown card
- **Leaving.** A player who goes back to the game list or loses the connection keeps their seat and can come back to it. Nothing about their cards is revealed, and the game waits for them if they must show a card. A player removed by a dismiss vote has their cards turned face up for everyone; their token stays on the board; if the game was waiting on them to show a card, the other holders carry on with the suggestion; if it was their turn, the turn passes on
- Any player can ask to undo the last move. It is undone only if every connected player approves. It cannot be asked right after a card was shown or an accusation was made, because the undo would reveal what was shown. Notes taken after an undone move are lost with it

## Controls

- On your turn click Roll, then click a highlighted square or room on the board. The highlighted squares are exactly those you can reach with the roll
- If the roll leads nowhere, click Stay put
- In a room with a secret passage, click Secret passage at the start of your turn to use it instead of rolling
- If a suggestion moved you into a room, click Stay in and the room's name at the start of your turn to stay there without rolling
- In a room, click Suggest, pick a suspect and an object, and click Make suggestion
- When a suggestion asks you to show a card and you have more than one that matches, click the card to show. The cards that do not match are not offered
- Click Accuse, pick a suspect, an object and a room, click Make accusation, then click Yes, accuse. Back returns to the pickers and Cancel closes them
- Click End turn when you are done
- The Suggestions list names the card you saw or showed; other suggestions show who could not show, and who showed a card
- Click a mark on the Notes sheet to cycle blank, yes, no and maybe. Notes stay when you reload the page
- Click Undo to ask everyone to undo the last move. The button is not shown right after a card was shown or an accusation
- Click Rules at the top of the page for these rules, and FAQ in it for a short guide to the rules and controls

## Differences from Wikipedia

These follow the classic rules in [Wikipedia's Cluedo article](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cluedo). What is different:

- **Setting, names and art.** This game has its own seaside hotel, six suspects, six objects and nine rooms with original names and drawings, and its own floor plan
- **Nine rooms, no central staircase.** The hotel has nine rooms in a three by three layout and no room in the middle, so there is no central staircase. Two secret passages join the four corner rooms in pairs
- **Movement.** Here the move is exactly the roll, with early entry to a room through a door, no square twice in one move, and squares holding another token blocked. The article is silent on these points
- **Stay put.** When the roll leads nowhere the player stays put. A player in a room whose doors are all blocked may still make a suggestion there
- **No revisits.** A room you started the turn in cannot be entered again on the same move
- **Who starts.** The first seat starts and turns go in seat order. The article does not say how the first player is chosen
- **The deal.** The 18 remaining cards are dealt one at a time from seat 1, so some hands are a card larger with 4 or 5 players. Seat 1 gets the extra cards first
- **One matching card.** A single matching card is shown at once, with no prompt, and a player with several chooses which
- **Objects move too.** The suggested object is moved into the room along with the suspect, and stays there
- **Notes.** The game keeps the notes sheet for each player, private to them, in place of a paper sheet
- **Leaving.** The article does not cover a player leaving. Here leaving holds the seat and reveals nothing, and a dismissed player's cards go face up for everyone
- **End of play.** A wrong accusation puts the player out of turns, but they still show cards. The last player still able to accuse wins at once, which the article does not cover. If every player is out, nobody wins and the answer is shown
- **Undo.** The article does not cover undoing. Here the table can vote to undo a move, except right after a card was shown or an accusation. Notes taken after an undone move are undone with it
